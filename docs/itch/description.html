Silk Road

A turn-based merchant game, 1247. Buy a ruined caravanserai and build a house whose mark is honoured from Tabriz to the Jade Gate.

Begin in Samarkand, Trebizond or Tabriz, each with its own roads, its own home market and its own rival houses. Buy what your city makes, load your beasts, and send someone you trust down a road that may not bring them back. Every season asks the same thing: how far, how much, and who leads it.

The game

  • 16 goods and 22 cities. A home bazaar only stocks what its own country makes; everything else has to be carried in, and a market you flood stops paying.
  • Caravans you plan load by load: which beasts, which escort, who leads. A son, a brother, a hired caravan master. The odds you see are the odds the game rolls.
  • A household with faces. Your sons, a brother if you bring one, the wives who marry in, and the people you hire each have a face of their own, and you see who would come before you hire them.
  • 15 merchant houses along the roads to marry into. Each match brings a dowry, a road or a trade for life, and one more pair of hands.
  • 3 rival houses on your roads in every start. Their caravans flood the markets you meant to sell into; strong enough, they outbid you, poach your staff and undercut you.
  • To win: 20,000 ducats, a name of 60 repute, and a caravan home from the far end of your own road. You lose if the house stays in the red too long: the creditors give you three turns, longer with a banking marriage. Winning is an ending, not a wall: you can keep trading.

About it

  • Plays in the browser, on a phone or a computer. Also a download: one file that opens in any browser and plays offline (not Safari: it will not keep saves from a file).
  • Saves in your browser, with three save slots and a copy-out text code that moves a game between devices.
  • Free. No account, no ads, no analytics. The page asks Google Fonts for its typefaces; without them it plays the same in your system's fonts.
